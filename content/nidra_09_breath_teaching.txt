Module five. The breath, and the gap.

<break time="2.0s" />

The Vijnana Bhairava Tantra, one of the oldest maps of meditation, points again and again to the breath. And especially, to the still point between the breaths.

<break time="1.5s" />

In this stage, you do not control the breath. You let it breathe itself, and you simply watch. The breath, flowing in. The breath, flowing out.

<break time="1.5s" />

A traditional way to steady the mind is to count the breaths, backwards. Breathing in, and out, twenty-seven. In, and out, twenty-six. And on. If you lose the count, you simply begin again.

<break time="1.5s" />

And then, the subtle teaching. Between each out-breath, and the next in-breath, there is a small pause. A gap. In that gap, for a moment, the breath is still. And so is the mind. The old texts say that here, in this quiet gap, something deep can be glimpsed.

<break time="1.5s" />

You do not force the pause. You only notice it. Resting there, for a moment, in the stillness, before the breath returns.

<break time="1.5s" />

In the practice, follow the breath, and rest in its quiet turning.
