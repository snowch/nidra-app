The depths.

<break time="2.0s" />

The eight limbs show the path as a journey forward. The ancient texts offer a second map. A journey inward, through the layers of what you are.

<break time="2.0s" />

The Taittiriya Upanishad describes five sheaths. Five koshas. Like vessels, one inside the next, each subtler than the last.

<break time="1.5s" />

The outermost is annamaya kosha, the sheath made of food. This is the physical body. The body you will make still.

<break time="1.5s" />

Within it lies pranamaya kosha, the sheath of energy, of breath and life-force. You touch this layer when you turn your attention to the breath.

<break time="1.5s" />

Deeper still is manomaya kosha, the sheath of the mind. Thoughts, sensations, emotions. The endless movement of the surface mind.

<break time="1.5s" />

Subtler again is vijnanamaya kosha, the sheath of wisdom. The quiet knowing that watches the mind, rather than being caught in it.

<break time="1.5s" />

And at the centre, anandamaya kosha, the sheath of bliss. A deep, causeless peace, that has nothing to do with anything happening.

<break time="2.0s" />

Yoga Nidra is a slow journey through these layers. From the body... to the breath... through the moving mind... to the one who watches... and toward that quiet centre.

<break time="1.5s" />

You do not have to force your way through. As the outer layers grow still, the subtler ones simply reveal themselves.

<break time="1.5s" />

So when you hear the word deeper, in this programme, this is what it means. Not further down into sleep, but further inward, through the sheaths, toward the stillness at your centre.

<break time="2.0s" />

Keep this map beside the first. The path shows where you are going. The sheaths show how deep it reaches.
