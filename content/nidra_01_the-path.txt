We begin with the path.

<break time="2.0s" />

Before we turn inward, it helps to know where we are going, and where this practice sits on the map.

<break time="2.0s" />

The sage Patanjali described the yogic path as eight limbs.

<break time="1.5s" />

The first four turn outward, and prepare the ground. Yama, how we live with others. Niyama, how we live with ourselves. Asana, a steady, comfortable posture. And pranayama, the steadying of the breath.

<break time="1.5s" />

The fifth limb is pratyahara, the withdrawal of the senses. It is the turning point. The moment attention stops flowing out toward the world, and begins to flow inward.

<break time="1.5s" />

And the final three turn fully inward. Dharana, concentration. Dhyana, meditation. And samadhi, absorption.

<break time="1.5s" />

So pratyahara sits in the middle. The doorway between the outer path, and the inner one.

<break time="2.0s" />

Now, where does Yoga Nidra sit?

<break time="1.5s" />

Here we must be honest. The eight limbs are Patanjali's map. Yoga Nidra grew from a different root, the tantric tradition. The two were woven together only much later.

<break time="1.5s" />

So we do not call nidra a limb. We say, rather, that nidra is a vehicle. It carries you across that middle doorway, through pratyahara, and into the inner limbs of concentration and absorption. Lying still, it opens a door that usually asks years of seated effort.

<break time="2.0s" />

This is why nidra is so prized. It does not demand that you first master the outer limbs. It offers the inner ones directly.

<break time="1.5s" />

And yet the outer limbs remain its ground. A settled life, a still body, a steady breath. These do not gate the practice, but they deepen it. You may strengthen them alongside, in your own time.

<break time="1.5s" />

As for the far end of the path. Nidra will carry you to the threshold of deep meditation, and let you taste it. The last steps, dhyana and samadhi in their fullness, are traditionally ripened in seated practice. Nidra opens the door. Seated stillness walks the rest of the way.

<break time="2.0s" />

For now, hold the simple shape of it. Prepare... withdraw... and turn inward. That inward turn is where our journey lives.
