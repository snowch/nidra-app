We begin with the path.

<break time="2.0s" />

Before we turn inward, it helps to know where we are going.

<break time="1.5s" />

The sage Patanjali described the yogic path as eight steps. And those steps fall into two halves.

<break time="1.5s" />

The first four face outward. They are the preparation. How we live, with others, and with ourselves. The steadiness of the body. And the calming of the breath. These settle the ground beneath us.

<break time="1.5s" />

Then comes the turning point, the fifth step. Here, attention stops flowing outward, towards the world, and begins to flow inward. And this is where Yoga Nidra begins.

<break time="1.5s" />

Beyond it, the final three steps lead deeper still. Inward, towards deep concentration, and stillness. Yoga Nidra will carry you a long way along this inward path.

<break time="1.5s" />

One honest word. Yoga Nidra did not itself come from this eight-step map. It grew from an older, tantric root. We borrow the map only to show you where nidra sits. At the doorway, between the outer world, and the inner one.

<break time="2.0s" />

So for now, simply hold the shape of it. First, we prepare... then, we turn inward... and then, we go deep. That inward turn, is where our journey lives.
