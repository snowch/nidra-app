Setting up your practice.

<break time="2.0s" />

A few practical words, before you begin.

<break time="1.5s" />

Yoga Nidra is practised lying down, on your back, in the posture called shavasana. Lie on a firm, comfortable surface. Let the feet fall open, the arms a little away from the body, and the palms turned upward.

<break time="1.5s" />

The body cools as it settles, so cover yourself with a blanket, and keep the room quiet, and dimly lit. If it helps you to relax, cover your eyes.

<break time="1.5s" />

Practise on an empty, or nearly empty, stomach. Not straight after a heavy meal. Any time of day will serve. Many find the early morning, or the early evening, especially still.

<break time="1.5s" />

And now, the one difficulty every practitioner meets. Sleep. Yoga Nidra walks the edge of sleep, and now and then, you will cross it. This is not a failure. If you drift off, simply begin again, where you can. Over time, the body learns to stay on the threshold. Deeply relaxed, but still aware.

<break time="1.5s" />

Lying flat can invite sleep more easily than sitting. If you find yourself dropping off, again and again, you might slightly raise the head, or keep the practice shorter, until the balance comes.

<break time="1.5s" />

That is all you need. A still place, a warm body, and the willingness to remain aware. With that, you are ready to begin.
