Pratyahara.

<break time="2.0s" />

Pratyahara is the fifth of the eight limbs of yoga, described by the sage Patanjali in the Yoga Sutras.

<break time="1.5s" />

The word comes from two parts. Prati, meaning away, or to withdraw. And ahara, meaning food, or anything we take in. So pratyahara is the withdrawal of the senses. Drawing your attention inward, away from the pull of the outside world.

<break time="1.5s" />

It sits right in the middle of the yogic path. The first four limbs prepare the body and the breath. The last three, concentration, meditation, and absorption, turn fully inward. Pratyahara is the bridge between them.

<break time="1.5s" />

It isn't about blocking the senses, or forcing sound and sensation away. The eyes still see. The ears still hear. But the mind simply stops chasing after what they bring. Like bees following the queen, the senses quietly follow the mind as it settles inward.

<break time="1.5s" />

In practice, this is what you are doing when you let sounds come and go without following them. When a thought appears, and you let it pass. When you rest your attention gently within yourself.

<break time="1.5s" />

With practice, you become less tossed around by every sound, craving, and distraction. And deeper concentration and meditation become possible.

<break time="2.0s" />

That, in essence, is pratyahara. The quiet art of turning inward.


<break time="1.5s" />

How will you know when you are ready to move on? When the senses begin to quiet, on their own... when you can let a sound, or a sensation, arise, and pass, without being pulled out towards it... and your attention rests, more and more easily, turned inward.

<break time="1.5s" />

There is no hurry. Stay with this, until it feels like your own.
