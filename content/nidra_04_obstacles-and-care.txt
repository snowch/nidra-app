Obstacles, and care.

<break time="2.0s" />

As you go deeper, a few things may arise. It helps to know them beforehand.

<break time="1.5s" />

The mind will wander. This is not a mistake. Each time you notice that you have drifted, you have, in that very moment, already returned. Gently come back, without judgement.

<break time="1.5s" />

The body may grow restless, or an itch may call for your attention. Where you can, let it be, and watch it pass. If you truly must move, move slowly, and return to stillness.

<break time="1.5s" />

And sometimes, as the body relaxes deeply, feelings, or memories, may rise to the surface. Old emotions, long held in the body, can release. If this happens, you do not need to chase them, nor push them away. Simply let them move through you, like weather, and return to the breath.

<break time="1.5s" />

A word of care. If you carry deep grief, or trauma, this deep relaxation can sometimes feel like too much. There is no need to force it. Keep the eyes softly open, if that feels safer. Go slowly. And seek the support of a teacher, or a professional, if strong feelings arise.

<break time="1.5s" />

Yoga Nidra is deeply restful. But it is not a treatment, and it is not a replacement for sleep, or for medical care.

<break time="1.5s" />

Above all, practise with kindness towards yourself. That gentleness is part of the practice.
