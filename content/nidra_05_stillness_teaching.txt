Module one. Stillness.

<break time="2.0s" />

Yoga Nidra does not begin with technique. It begins with stillness.

<break time="1.5s" />

In the old texts, the practitioner lies down like a corpse, in the posture called shavasana, and becomes completely, deliberately still.

<break time="1.5s" />

The Mandukya Upanishad describes four states of consciousness. Waking. Dreaming. Deep sleep. And turiya, the aware fourth that underlies them all.

<break time="1.5s" />

Yoga Nidra walks the narrow edge between waking and sleep. The body enters the stillness of deep sleep, but you, the witness, remain awake.

<break time="1.5s" />

So in this first practice you are learning only one thing. To be completely still, and to stay aware at the edge of sleep.

<break time="1.5s" />

That stillness is the ground on which everything else is built.

<break time="1.5s" />

How will you know when you are ready to move on? When you can lie down, and let the body grow still, within a breath or two... and rest at that quiet edge for the whole practice, without fidgeting, and without drifting off to sleep... and when you can find that stillness yourself, without leaning on every word. When the practice stops feeling like an instruction, and becomes, simply, a place you can go.

<break time="1.5s" />

There is no hurry. For most, that is a few days, to a week. Stay here, until it feels like your own.

<break time="2.0s" />

When you are ready, find a quiet place to lie down, and begin the short practice.
