Module six. The pairs of opposites.

<break time="2.0s" />

The Bhagavad Gita teaches equanimity. To meet heat and cold, pleasure and pain, as they come and go, without being tossed about by them. The one who is steady among the pairs of opposites, it says, is ready for what is deathless.

<break time="1.5s" />

In Yoga Nidra, we practise this directly. We call up opposite sensations, one after another, and feel each one fully. Heaviness, then lightness. Warmth, then coolness.

<break time="1.5s" />

By feeling both, and being shaken by neither, the nervous system comes into balance, and the mind learns a deep evenness. We are no longer pulled towards the pleasant, nor away from the unpleasant. We simply feel. And remain still.

<break time="1.5s" />

This is not imagination alone. As you evoke each sensation, the body itself often begins, faintly, to feel it. Heaviness, sinking. Lightness, rising. You feel it. You release it. And you turn to meet its opposite.

<break time="1.5s" />

In the practice, let each sensation arise as fully as it will. Then let it dissolve, and turn to its opposite.


<break time="1.5s" />

How will you know when you are ready to move on? When you can call up each sensation, and feel it fully... then let it go, and turn to meet its opposite... feeling both, and shaken by neither... resting, even, in the middle of them.

<break time="1.5s" />

There is no hurry. Stay with this, until it feels like your own.
