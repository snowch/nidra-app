Module seven. Inner space.

<break time="2.0s" />

Behind the closed eyes, there is a dark space. The old texts call it chidakasha. The space of consciousness. The inner sky, in which all images, thoughts, and colours appear.

<break time="1.5s" />

This is the field of dharana. Of concentration. And here, Yoga Nidra draws on the tantric practice of resting attention in empty space, and watching whatever arises within it.

<break time="1.5s" />

Gently rest your awareness in that dark space, behind the forehead. You may see only darkness. You may see faint colours, or drifting images. Whatever appears, you neither hold it, nor push it away. You simply watch it arise, and dissolve, in the space.

<break time="1.5s" />

Sometimes, a single image is offered, to hold. A still lake. A candle flame. A point of light. You rest your attention there, gathering the scattered mind into one place.

<break time="1.5s" />

But the images are not the goal. The goal is the space itself. Vast, dark, and still. In which everything comes, and goes.

<break time="1.5s" />

In the practice, rest in the inner space, and let it hold whatever appears.


<break time="1.5s" />

How will you know when you are ready to move on? When you can rest in the dark inner space, at ease... letting colours, and images, come, and go... without chasing them, and without trying to make anything happen.

<break time="1.5s" />

There is no hurry. Stay with this, until it feels like your own.
